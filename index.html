    About this data

    How to read this

    • Pick a state in the box at the top. Every number on the page switches to that state.
    • Yellow is urban, orange is rural. The urban bar is always on top and the rural bar below it, and each value is printed as a number, so you never need the colours alone.
    • View 1: a longer bar means more mothers or children got that care. A shorter orange bar means rural families miss out.
    • Click any stage in view 1 to compare that stage with India and neighbouring states in view 2.
    • View 2 shows whether this state's gap is unusual. The neighbour with the smallest gap is marked as a model to learn from.
    • Views 3 and 4 give the outcome context (infant and newborn deaths) and the cost families still carry.
    • Linking stages to schemes: births in a health facility relate to Janani Suraksha Yojana, and child vaccination to Mission Indradhanush, so a wide gap at one stage points to the scheme worth studying.

    What this doesn’t show

    Gaps and collection method

    • These are survey estimates from NFHS-5 household interviews, not hospital records. Each number has a margin of error.
    • Fieldwork ran in two phases between 2019 and 2021, and the second phase was paused by COVID-19. Things may have changed since.
    • Numbers in ( ) are based on only 25–49 cases and are less reliable. Cells shown as – were hidden by the survey because fewer than 25 cases were available. Nothing hidden has been estimated or filled in.
    • Maternal care indicators describe each mother's most recent birth in the five years before the survey.
    • There is no district detail in this file. A state average can hide districts that are far behind.

    Who is missing, and what not to conclude

    • The survey covers people living in households. People in institutions, homeless families and many migrants are not counted.
    • Chandigarh has no rural figures. Lakshadweep and some small states have hidden death rates.
    • Rural vs urban is all this view compares. It does not show gaps by caste, income, tribe or religion.
    • View 3 sits next to views 1–2, but it does not prove that more care causes fewer deaths. Many other things affect infant deaths.
    • It does not show health budgets or scheme spending, so it cannot explain why a gap exists, only where it is widest.
